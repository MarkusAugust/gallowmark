## What it is

In the world, LineLord is a ledger that cannot be argued with. Put a thing in
front of it, a wall, a law, a song, and it says who laid each course of it and
how long that course has stood. It does not say whether the wall is good. It
does not say who worked hardest. It counts what is there and when it was last
touched, and then it is quiet.

The [[river-priests]] named it, as they name everything, and then said nothing
more about it. [[gorvek]] carries it. He does not read, and does not need to:
the ledger is read to him, and he says what he thinks of it. Every word LineLord
speaks is his, which is why it speaks in creeds and never explains.

Outside the world, LineLord is a program that reads `git blame` and reports who
owns which line. This entry is where the two meet. The voice on its screens is
Gorvek's; the names on them are Gallowmark's.

## The rule

LineLord counts lines and dates and nothing else. It does not count deeds, it
does not know what a line does or how hard it was to lay, and it says nothing of
the worth of the one who laid it. Old work is work nobody has touched, which is
not the same as good work. The [[river-priests]] hold that this is why the
ledger is allowed to exist at all: a count that judged would be a lie, and a
lie has no name.

## The legends

The stories say the ledger is older than [[the-ashfall]], that the river-priests
kept their most terrible craft by it in the grey, and that the last king of
[[kell]] sent for it the night [[sarn]] took the rubies, wanting to know whose
hand had laid the quays. Whether it answered him nobody says. Kell went under
either way.
